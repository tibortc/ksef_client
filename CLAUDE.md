  5. ~~The parser~~ — **done 2026-08-24**, then hardened the same day by a five-agent audit that found real defects (see below). `lib/ksef/fa3/parser.rb` plus `row_reader.rb`, `node_reader.rb`, `element_tree.rb`, `rounding_inference.rb`, `provenance.rb`, `document_mapping.rb` and `canonical.rb`, with `Ksef::FA3.parse`. DESIGN.md §7.6's round-trip law runs green over a pinned corpus of upstream samples (`spec/ksef/fa3/round_trip_spec.rb`). Five things here are load-bearing and should not be "tidied":
     - **`P_11` is read, never recomputed.** Upstream's own corpus has a row of `20 × 1000` whose net is `18000` — a discounted line. Deriving the net would silently rewrite an invoice.
     - **The parser does not validate and does not check a NIP's checksum.** Reading a document to find out *why* KSeF rejected it is a normal thing to want, so parsing must not refuse bad input. (And per §15.3, only PROD checks NIP digits at all.)
     - **`#raw_document` is retained and excluded from identity**; `#unmapped_elements` names what `#to_xml` would drop, computed by *difference* against the serializer so it cannot drift. `parse` → `to_xml` is lossy for any document richer than the model, and that has to be visible rather than merely recoverable.
     - **The model stores the document's representation, not the caller's input** (`docs/REFERENCE.md` §8.2b). `Address` composes `street`/`city`/`postal_code` into `line1` at construction and drops the parts; `Line` converts amounts to `BigDecimal` **and rounds them to their element's scale**; `Invoice` renders `issued_at` to its string form and coerces `issue_date` to a `Date`. This is what makes `parse(invoice.to_xml) == invoice` literally true, and it was checked against the only official FA(3) reader (`ksef-pdf-generator`) rather than invented. The one honest exception is `rounding`, which the document does not record at all — so a `:per_summary` invoice whose summaries happen to equal `:per_line`'s comes back as `:per_line`.
     - **`Adnotacje` are read, not defaulted.** `Invoice#annotations` carries what the document said. Emitting `DEFAULT_ANNOTATIONS` regardless silently denied every declaration an invoice made — cash accounting, reverse charge, split payment, a real VAT exemption — and `#unmapped_elements` is blind to it, because the element paths are identical either way. **That is the shape of the whole bug class:** a path-difference diagnostic cannot see a changed *value*, so anything with tax meaning has to be a field the model carries.
     - **What the audit fixed, so it is not re-broken:** summaries now *accumulate* per bucket (several rate codes share one — §8.1a — and assigning let the last win, understating the tax base on an XSD-valid document); `Validator` checks well-formedness, since libxml2 recovers by default and a recovered tree validates clean; `parse` refuses a `RodzajFaktury` the model does not carry rather than emitting a plausible impostor under the original's number (`VAT` and `KOR` are carried as of 2026-08-24); `P_7` and `Podmiot2/Adres` are optional as the XSD says; `Date`/`BigDecimal` failures surface as `ValidationError`; and `FA3::Canonical` routes `#with` through the constructor, because **`Data#with` skips a custom `initialize` on Ruby 3.2** and every invariant was bypassable on the declared floor.

Everything binding is in `docs/REFERENCE.md` §8.4, but four things in particular:
     - **A correction's summaries are read, never computed.** Its buckets are *deltas*, and FA(3) does not require its rows to determine them — Przykład 5 and 6 have no `FaWiersz` at all, Przykład 7's row carries no amount. So `Invoice#totals` holds what the document states, keyed by **element name** because rate code → bucket is not invertible (§8.1a), and `lines` may be empty **only** when totals are stated. The after − before rule that Przykład 2 satisfies is **deliberately not implemented**: one witness is not a rule, and deriving a tax base from an inference is what §15.6 warns against.

Przykład 2 gives a before/after pair the *same* `NrWierszaFa`, which after `UU_ID` is dropped is the only thing pairing them — but storing the number unconditionally would make a parsed ordinary invoice unequal to the built invoice it came from, breaking §7.6.
     - **Do not CRC-check `NrKSeFFaKorygowanej`** (§8.4a). All six KSeF numbers in the Ministry's own corrections fail §13's checksum; they are well-formed placeholders.   9. ~~`UPR` and the two `KOR_` combinations~~ — **done 2026-08-26. All seven types are modelled**, and twenty-two of the twenty-six samples parse, re-serialise, validate and round-trip; the four that do not are refused for a *construct* (two priced gross, two non-NIP buyers), not a type. `KOR_ZAL` and `KOR_ROZ` needed one element between them, `P_15ZK`; everything else was already built. `UPR` needed the real change — **a row that states no amount at all** — and everything binding is in `docs/REFERENCE.md` §8.6:
     - **`Line#net` answers nil rather than raising.** Every child of `FaWiersz` but `NrWierszaFa` is `minOccurs="0"`, so an amount-less row is the schema's default and the model was the strict one. nil is not zero: the row states nothing, rather than stating it is worth nothing. The same change unblocked **Przykład 7**, the last Ministry correction this model could not read, so all five now go through.
     - **The parser stopped refusing two shapes and tier 1 took them over.** An unpriced row, or one with an amount and no `P_12`, is legal — but on an invoice that *derives* its summary the amount is simply absent from the tax base, and **neither tier can see that**: the XSD is blind to arithmetic and `#unmapped_elements` to values. So the rule is line-addressed and scoped to `totals.nil?`. `net_by_rate` skips what it cannot place, which is exactly why the rule is needed.
     - **Gross pricing is still refused at parse time**, and the distinction matters: a gross-priced row carries a number the model has nowhere to put, so reading it would drop a real amount. An unpriced row has no number to drop.
     - **`P_15ZK` means two different things** — the amount *paid* before correction on a `KOR_ZAL`, the amount *left to pay* on a `KOR_ROZ`. One element, two readings, and the type decides. `Correction#paid_before` carries the figure without naming it more precisely than the schema does.
     - **What the five-lens audit found, so it is not re-broken.** Three defects, all of the same family — *a number the model quietly changed*: `P_9A`/`P_9AZ` are **`TKwotowy2` (8 places), not `TKwotowy`**, so reading a unit price at two silently altered a stated amount on a valid document (§8.6, and `Formatting::UNIT_PRICE_SCALE` now exists); `KursWalutyZK` was the one field not rounded to its element's scale, so a `Correction` stopped equalling itself through a round-trip; and `Line#vat` answered a definite `0` where the tax is *unknown*, while `#net` and `#gross` answered nil. Two test holes went with them: **`P_9B` alone was never exercised** — both Ministry gross samples carry `P_11A` too, and an `||` operand is not a branch either coverage criterion can see — and `#vat_rounded_per_line`'s skip guard was the round's one uncovered branch. Also: `MINISTRY_UNSUPPORTED_TYPES` was asserted against its own definition and passed with `UPR` removed from `SUPPORTED_TYPES`; it is measured now. **And `TFaWiersz` does not exist** — `FaWiersz` has an anonymous complexType, FA(3) names only seven — which nine files asserted.
     - **The guard class changed when the parser stopped refusing, and that is the thing to remember.** An unpriced or rateless row used to be *impossible*; it is now *reported if you ask*. Delete one `P_12` from Przykład 1 and the re-serialised `P_15` is 50.01 lower, XSD-clean both ways, `#unmapped_elements` silent, **and the round-trip law still true** — because a parsed invoice is already a fixed point, so §7.6 cannot see it. Only `#errors` can. The facade validates by default; `#to_xml` on its own does not.
  10. ~~Validator tier 3 (§7.7)~~ — **done 2026-08-26**, then redesigned the same day after a five-lens audit found the first version refused legal invoices. `BusinessValidator`, reached through **`Invoice#warnings`** — deliberately *not* `#errors`, so it can never block a send (`docs/REFERENCE.md` §17). Four things bind:
     - **It is a warning, and that is the whole design.** A Polish invoice priced from round gross prices legitimately misses `Σ buckets == P_15` by a grosz *per line*. Making that an error would repeat exactly what got KSeF's own proposed business rule withdrawn (§15.6, issue #837: *it rejects legal invoices*). §14.3 is the precedent.
     - **Both sides of the comparison come from the document**, never from the model's derivation. The first version compared derived buckets against a stated total and produced two false positives that `Client#send_invoice` turned into refusals: a rounding-regime artefact (buckets rounded, `gross_total` not — three ordinary lines gave a two-grosz "error" on an invoice this gem had just built), and an accusation against any document using `P_13_5`/`P_14_5`/`P_13_11`, buckets no rate code reaches. A **built** invoice states nothing independently and is therefore never compared.
     - **The tolerance is one grosz and honestly arbitrary.** §1.5's explanation of Przykład 1 was wrong twice — it is neither gross pricing nor per-bucket tax rounding (which moves that invoice by 0.0007) but nets computed back *w stu* from round gross prices and rounded down. So the error scales with **line count**, and no tolerance sized from a 26-sample corpus can be sound. Also: **three** samples state no buckets (5, 13, 16), not one, so the rule evaluates 23 of 26.
     - **Grounding is empirical, not definitional** (§15.6 grounding 3). `P_15`'s annotation says nothing about the buckets, and the definitional alternative — check `P_13_1` against the rows — is falsified by ten of the fourteen stated-summary samples. **But the XSD does state one equality**, `Rozliczenie/DoZaplaty = P_15 + Obciazenia − Odliczenia`, which holds exactly in all four corpus samples carrying it and is unimplemented only because the model does not carry `Rozliczenie` (§17.4). §15.6 said "They state no equalities" and was wrong.
     - Along the way: **`P_15` was derived rather than read** on a `VAT` invoice, so re-serialising Przykład 1 made it a grosz cheaper with every diagnostic silent (§17.2). `Invoice#stated_gross` carries it when it differs, canonicalised **in the constructor** — the parser-only version let `Invoice.new` and `#with` break §7.6.
  11. ~~`docs/field_mapping.md`~~ — **done 2026-08-26.** `rake fa3:field_mapping` renders it from a declared mapping in `tasks/field_mapping.rb` plus the pinned XSD; `rake fa3:verify` fails on a stale file, the same gate `generated/` gets. Two things bind:
     - **The declaration is the only hand-written part.** Element names, types, cardinalities and the Ministry's Polish descriptions are all looked up from the schema at render time. Three guards abort the build rather than letting the table lie: an unresolvable element path, an attribute that is not a member of its model, and a model member that is neither mapped nor given a reason in `UNMAPPED`. **Add a field to a model and the build fails until you say where it goes** — that is the whole mechanism §7.2 asked for.
     - **Descriptions are shown only where every occurrence of an element name agrees.** Eleven names are declared with different wording in different places (`DaneIdentyfikacyjne` has five variants, `Adres` four); guessing one would put the wrong statute against a field an auditor is reading. An element with no `type` renders as *(inline)* rather than getting a name invented for it — FA(3) names only seven complexTypes, and inventing one is how `TFaWiersz` came to be asserted in nine files.
     - **What the audit changed, so it is not undone.** The first version read cardinality from `Generated::Types`, which is **flattened** for the serializer's benefit — so it reported the buyer's `NIP` and `Nazwa` as mandatory when the first is one branch of a choice and the second sits in an optional sequence. That is §8.2a's three-time bug, printed in a table for auditors. It now walks the XSD itself, which also makes descriptions **path-exact** (so `Adres` and `KodKraju` are no longer blank) and let the truncation go entirely — truncating cut mid-citation, because every Polish statutory abbreviation ends in `". "`, and it dropped *"W przypadku faktur korygujących"* from ten of the eighteen buckets, which is where a correction's amounts are defined as **deltas**. `Podmiot1` and `Podmiot2` are different XSD types and now get a section each.
     - **One language per column.** The Ministry's `xsd:documentation` is quoted in Polish, verbatim and unabridged, in a column labelled as such; everything this project wrote is English, in a `Notes` column. The first version mixed our English prose into the Polish column for the rows that have no single element, so a reader could not tell whose sentence was whose — bad in any table, worse in one whose whole claim is that the Polish is quoted rather than paraphrased. **Do not translate it:** it carries statutory citations, and a paraphrase of a tax rule is a different tax rule.
     - **The guards check that both ends exist, not that they correspond.** Swapping `number → P_1` with `issue_date → P_2` passed every one of them. A spec now checks the pairing against the Ministry's documents, and another asserts every `Data` value object under `Ksef::FA3` is either mapped or named as not carrying invoice fields — a whole model class could previously be omitted in silence.

## Commands

Every command below needs this prelude first:

```bash
export PATH="$HOME/.rbenv/shims:$PATH" LANG=en_US.UTF-8
```

rbenv's shims are not on `PATH` in a non-interactive shell — without the prefix, `bundle` resolves to macOS system Ruby 2.6.10 and dies. Without `LANG`, `File.read`/`JSON.parse` raise `Encoding::InvalidByteSequenceError` on the Polish text that pervades the KSeF specs and schemas.

- `bundle exec rake` — **the one to reach for.** Runs `verify:artifacts`, `fa3:verify`, `spec`, `rubocop`. This is the definition of done.
- `bundle exec rake verify:artifacts` — check pinned artifacts against `docs/artifacts.sha256`. A failure means **upstream changed**: investigate and ledger it, never "repair" the checkout.
- `bundle exec rspec` — unit + golden tests. Must never touch the network; `spec_helper` enforces that via WebMock.
- `bundle exec rspec spec/ksef/configuration_spec.rb:42` — single example while iterating.
- `bundle exec rubocop` — style authority. Not done until clean; prefer fixing over disabling cops.
- `KSEF_INTEGRATION=1 KSEF_ENV=test bundle exec rspec --tag integration` — live TEST suite. **Both** the env var and the tag are needed: RSpec ANDs exclusion filters with CLI inclusions, so the variable is what lifts the exclusion. Run only when explicitly asked; it is nightly-CI's job, and it reaches the network and consumes shared TEST state. `spec/integration/auth_flow_spec.rb` provisions a fresh test person per run rather than reusing `KSEF_TEST_NIP`, because re-authenticating an existing context by XAdES needs the PESEL that holds its permissions and that is not among the stored secrets.
- `KSEF_RELEASE_CHECK=1 bundle exec rspec` — adds the release gates: `spec/release_readiness_spec.rb` (gemspec invariants, the four approved runtime deps, no reintroduced metadata placeholders) and `spec/recorded_tier_spec.rb`'s cassette check. **Currently green — keep it that way.** The cassette gate was red by design from the harness landing until the recording; if it goes red again, `spec/cassettes/` has been emptied.

- `bundle exec rake auth:bootstrap` — **one-time, human-run.** Provisions a TEST credential: invents a checksum-valid NIP/PESEL, registers via the unauthenticated `/testdata/person`, authenticates by XAdES with a self-signed certificate, mints a KSeF token, and prints `KSEF_TEST_NIP` / `KSEF_TEST_TOKEN` for the **environment** secrets on `ksef-test` — not repository secrets, which every workflow in the repo can read (§6a.3). Refuses anything but TEST. The token it prints is a real credential — it goes straight into GitHub secrets, never into a file here. Logic lives in `tasks/ksef_bootstrap.rb` and is spec-covered against stubs (`docs/REFERENCE.md` §6a.3).
- `bundle exec rake fa3:generate` — regenerate `lib/ksef/fa3/generated/` from the pinned XSD. Output is **committed**; never hand-edit it. The generator lives in `tasks/fa3_generator.rb`, outside `lib/` so it is not packaged.
- `bundle exec rake fa3:verify` — regenerate and fail on any byte difference. Catches both a non-deterministic generator and a stale committed `generated/`. Part of the default task and of CI.

## Verifying the Ruby 3.2 floor

**RuboCop cannot do this, and must not be trusted to.** `TargetRubyVersion: 3.2` catches *syntax* — it does flag the `it` block parameter. It does **not** flag post-3.2 APIs: `Range#overlap?` (3.3), `Array#fetch_values` (3.4) and `MatchData#bytebegin` (3.4) all pass clean under this project's config. Relying on RuboCop here ships a gem that `NoMethodError`s on the declared floor, and the failure lands on users rather than CI.

Only running on 3.2 proves it. **Ruby 3.2.11 is installed**, so run it before declaring any milestone done (DESIGN.md §10):

```bash
export PATH="$HOME/.rbenv/shims:$PATH" LANG=en_US.UTF-8
mv Gemfile.lock /tmp/Gemfile.lock.dev                 # lockfile is resolved under 4.0.6
BUNDLE_PATH=/tmp/ksef-bundle-32 RBENV_VERSION=3.2.11 bundle install
BUNDLE_PATH=/tmp/ksef-bundle-32 RBENV_VERSION=3.2.11 bundle exec rake
mv /tmp/Gemfile.lock.dev Gemfile.lock                 # restore
```

The separate `BUNDLE_PATH` keeps the 4.0.6 gem set intact, and resolving without the lockfile mirrors what CI does per matrix Ruby. Bundler under 3.2.11 is 2.4.19, which cannot read a lockfile written by Bundler 4.

Last verified green on 3.2.11 (**2026-08-26**, after the retrieval cassette): 1564 examples, 0 failures, line 100 / branch 98.75 / method 100, RuboCop clean, `rake exit=0`. Keep this line current — it went four milestones stale before an audit noticed, which is exactly the failure "run it before declaring any milestone done" exists to prevent. **Check `rake`'s exit code, not just its output** — a coverage-floor failure prints among the RuboCop lines and is easy to read past; `rake exit=0` is the only reliable signal. This is how the skipped-gate bug described under **Workflow** below stayed hidden. The crypto primitives were measured on both 3.2.11 and 4.0.6 before any code was written — `OpenSSL::PKey::PKey#encrypt` with explicit OAEP options, the NIST and FIPS vectors, and the 190-byte OAEP limit all behave identically. One difference worth knowing: a wrong-padding decrypt through **`RSA#private_decrypt`** raises `OpenSSL::PKey::PKeyError` on 4.0.6 but `OpenSSL::PKey::RSAError` on 3.2.11. Through `PKey#decrypt` — which is what this gem actually calls — both raise `PKeyError`, so the divergence does not bite the code as written. **Rescue `OpenSSL::OpenSSLError`** in any spec asserting a crypto failure regardless: it costs nothing and the openssl *gem* version varies independently of Ruby's.

Even so, when reaching for any core or stdlib method, confirm it exists in 3.2 rather than assuming — a filtered local run will not catch it.

> **Settled 2026-08-22 — do not re-raise.** Ruby 3.2 is EOL, and it stays the floor anyway. The version-support policy's EOL-drop rule governs future series (3.3 onward), not this floor; the reasoning is in DESIGN.md §3. Do not propose raising the floor to 3.3, and do not treat 3.2's EOL as licence to use post-3.2 APIs.

## Hard rules — violating any of these means stop and flag

- Never invent KSeF endpoint paths, XML element names, namespace URIs, or crypto parameters. Verify against the pinned OpenAPI spec / XSD / CIRFMF docs and record value + source + date in `docs/REFERENCE.md` first.
- Never target the PROD environment from any test or script. Integration code must hard-fail on `KSEF_ENV=prod`.
- **Never interpolate `${{ inputs.* }}` into a workflow `run:` script** — it is substituted before the shell parses, and `record-cassettes.yml` exports a live KSeF credential on the step that had it. Pass through `env:` and reference `"$VAR"`. Same rule in Ruby: `sh` with one string goes through `/bin/sh`, so pass argv entries.
- **Only GET and HEAD are ever auto-retried.** Every POST/PUT/PATCH/DELETE surfaces its error to the caller — a duplicate invoice in KSeF is a real tax problem. Enforced by `Ksef::HTTP::Retry`, which delegates every decision to `Ksef::RetryPolicy#retryable?` (DESIGN.md §6.7). **Until 2026-08-23 nothing consulted the policy at all** — it was built, validated, documented as a `retry:` option and never called, so `Retry-After` was honoured nowhere. If you touch the middleware, keep the method check in the policy rather than the middleware: one place decides. **Sole ledgered exception:** the opt-in `21470` key-rotation remediation in `Crypto::PublicKeys#with_key_rotation`, where the request was declined outright so nothing happened server-side and the retry carries a *different* key (`docs/REFERENCE.md` §10.2). Not a precedent for anything else.
- Honour `Retry-After` unclamped. Retrying sooner than instructed lengthens the block, and KSeF treats rotating IPs to dodge a 429 as an abuse pattern.
- No secrets in code, logs, fixtures, or VCR cassettes — scrub filters per DESIGN.md §4.5. **A scanner only finds the shapes someone listed**, so when §9.1 names a value as secret, something must scan for it: a live pre-signed Azure SAS sat in two committed cassettes past all four checks, because a SAS `sig` is not `Bearer`-prefixed, not JWT-shaped, and not an env value (2026-08-26). **Scan decoded bodies, not the file.** Psych stores a body carrying any non-ASCII byte as `!binary`, so a secret inside one is invisible to a regex over the cassette and to `include?` of a known value — and `Łódź` in a seller name is routine here. `spec/cassette_hygiene_spec.rb` decodes first and keeps a planted secret to prove it still works.
- No new runtime dependency beyond the four in DESIGN.md §4.3 (`faraday`, `nokogiri`, `bigdecimal`, `zeitwerk`) without asking. Excluded: `base64`, `logger`/`ostruct` requires, `jwt`, `activesupport`, `dry-*`, `rexml`. `rubyzip` is deferred to 0.2, not permanently banned.
- **Two upstream traps that only bite at runtime. Both now have code**, so these are maintenance rules rather than warnings about future work. `docs/REFERENCE.md` §14.1: the AES IV is a discrete request field, *not* a prefix on the ciphertext, whatever `sesja-interaktywna.md` says — the prose version yields payloads KSeF cannot decrypt; implemented in `Crypto::Encryptor`. §14.2: `downloadUrl` is a pre-signed storage link — follow it as an opaque URI, **never send the bearer token to it**, and verify `x-ms-meta-hash`; implemented in `UPO::Client`, which routes those requests over `HTTP::Connection.storage` — a connection with no bearer and no base URL, so the rule cannot be broken rather than merely being remembered (§12.3). **Do not "simplify" that into the main connection.** `spec/openapi_contract_spec.rb` guards both against the contract; read the ledger before changing either.
- No `Float` anywhere money flows — `BigDecimal` only.
- `required_ruby_version` stays `">= 3.2.0"` — no upper bound, ever.
- Do not commit `Gemfile.lock` (library convention; CI resolves per matrix Ruby).

## Conventions

- Ruby floor is 3.2: no `it` block parameter, no 3.3+/4.0-only syntax **and no post-3.2 APIs**. RuboCop only half-covers this — see the floor section above.
- `# frozen_string_literal: true` in every file. `Data.define` for value objects. `pack("m0")` / `unpack1("m0")` for base64.
- Always pass `encoding: "UTF-8"` to `File.read`. The ambient locale is not UTF-8, and every KSeF artifact contains Polish characters.
- **Where a pinned schema lives is a licensing decision, not a layout one.** `lib/` only for the Ministry's own documents (FA(3), auth, UPO — MIT, so redistributable) *and* only when something at runtime reads them, **or will within the current milestone** — the UPO schema was pinned ahead of its consumer under that amendment, and `UPO::Validator` now reads it at runtime, so the rule's plain form applies again (`docs/REFERENCE.md` §1.3 records the precedent for the next time). Third-party schemas it merely redistributes (W3C xmldsig, ETSI XAdES, OASIS UBL) go in `spec/fixtures/` and are not packaged: MF's licence cannot relicense someone else's document. `docs/REFERENCE.md` §1.2 has the test to apply; moving one into `lib/` is a question for the human (DESIGN.md §12).
- Every behavior change lands with specs. FA(3) serializer changes land with golden-file updates, and goldens must validate against the pinned XSD.
- Coverage is gated on **line 99, branch 98, method 100** (`generated/` excluded) — the gate is the `minimum_coverage` call in `spec/spec_helper.rb`, and **that file is the authority**; branch went 95 → 96 → 97 on 2026-08-24 and **97 → 98 at the Phase 2 boundary on 2026-08-26**. Five documents once kept saying 95 until an audit caught it, so grep for the number when you change it. **Re-ratchet these at each phase boundary, not once** — they are percentages, so the absolute number of untested branches they permit grows as the codebase does; Phase 2 roughly doubles it. Part of the phase definition of done. The floors ratchet — raise them when the real numbers improve, never lower one to make a change pass. `method: 100` is the one that bites: a method nothing exercises fails the build. Branch coverage is where real gaps hide, so a change that adds a conditional needs a test per path, not just per line. Filtered runs (single file, `--tag`) skip the gate by design.
- Thread safety of `Ksef::Client` is a requirement (DESIGN.md §5.2), not an optimization — and it is now shipped and spec-covered: frozen config, stateless connections, one mutex round the memoised credential, no session held on the client. A spec drives six concurrent sends and asserts a single authentication.
